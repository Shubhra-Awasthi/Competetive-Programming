## 1. INTRODUCTION

### What is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is the art of communicating clearly with AI models (like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude). Just as you would brief a colleague on a task, you need to brief the AI. The better your briefing (prompt), the better the result.

### Why This Matters for Us

Standard prompts often yield generic results. By applying the techniques in this guide, we can:

- **Reduce Errors:** Get accurate answers the first time.
- **Save Time:** Automate repetitive writing, summarizing, and data tasks.
- **Standardize Outputs:** Ensure consistent formatting across the company.

---

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLES (The "Basics")

To get the best results, every prompt should ideally contain these four elements:

1.  **Instruction:** What do you want the AI to do? (e.g., "Summarize", "Translate", "Classify")
2.  **Context:** Who is this for? What is the background? (e.g., "For a client meeting", "Acting as a senior engineer")
3.  **Input Data:** The content you want it to process.
4.  **Output Indicator:** How do you want the answer to look? (e.g., "Bullet points", "A table", "JSON format")

### Top Tips for Success

- **Be Specific:** Instead of saying "Write a short email," say "Write a 3-sentence email to a client apologizing for the delay."
- **Focus on "Do's" not "Don'ts":** Tell the AI what it _should_ do. Positive instructions are followed more accurately than negative ones.
- **Iterate:** Your first prompt might not be perfect. Refine it by adding constraints (e.g., "Make it shorter", "Use a friendlier tone").

## 3. KEY TECHNIQUES

You don't need to be technical to use these powerful methods.

### A. Zero-Shot Prompting (Direct Ask)

_Used for simple, quick tasks where the AI likely already knows the answer._

> **Prompt:** "Classify the sentiment of this text: 'The service was slow but the food was great.' Sentiment:"
> **Output:** Neutral

### B. Few-Shot Prompting (The "Example" Method)

_Best for: Ensuring the AI follows a specific format or style. You give the AI examples of what you want before asking it to do the work._

> **Prompt:**
> "Classify the priority of these tickets:
> Issue: 'The site is down.' -> Priority: High
> Issue: 'Typo on the homepage.' -> Priority: Low
> Issue: 'I cannot log in.' -> Priority:"
> **Output:** High

### C. Chain-of-Thought (Step-by-Step)

_Best for: Math, logic, or complex reasoning._

> **Prompt:** "The odd numbers in this group add up to an even number: 15, 32, 5, 13, 82, 7, 1. Let's think step by step."
> **Output:** The AI will break down the math instead of guessing, leading to the correct answer.

## 4. REAL-WORLD IMPACT: A CASE STUDY

**The Scenario:** A company needed to classify job descriptions to see if they were suitable for recent graduates ("Entry Level").

- **The Basic Approach:** They simply asked the AI, "Is this job entry level?"
  - _Result:_ **65% Accuracy**. The AI often got confused by "years of experience" requirements.
- **The Engineered Approach:** They modified the prompt to:
  1.  Give the AI a role ("You are a Recruiter").
  2.  Repeat key instructions.
  3.  Explain _why_ a job is entry-level.
  - _Result:_ **92% Accuracy**.

**Takeaway:** Spending 2 minutes to craft a better prompt can double the quality of your output.

## 5. PROMPT TEMPLATES LIBRARY

_Copy, paste, and fill in the bracketed information `[ ]` for your tasks._

**Use Case:** Polishing rough notes into a professional email.

> **Template:**
> "You are a professional executive assistant.
> **Task:** Rewrite the draft below into a polite, professional email to a client.
> **Tone:** Professional but warm.
> **Draft:** [Paste your rough notes here, e.g., 'Sorry missed meeting, stuck in traffic, reschedule for Tuesday?']
> **Output Format:** Standard email format with subject line."

### Category: Meetings & Summarization

**Use Case:** Turning messy transcripts into action items.

> **Template:**
> "You are an expert project manager.
> **Context:** The text below is a transcript from our weekly team sync.
> **Task:** Provide a summary of the meeting.
> **Output Format:**
>
> 1. Key Decisions Made
> 2. Action Items (Who needs to do what)
> 3. Open Questions
>    **Input Text:** [Paste meeting transcript or notes here]"

### Category: Customer Support (Classification)

**Use Case:** Quickly sorting customer feedback.

> **Template:**
> "Classify the following customer reviews into categories: 'Shipping Issue', 'Product Defect', or 'Praise'.
>
> **Examples:**
> Review: 'It arrived broken.' -> Category: Product Defect
> Review: 'I love the color!' -> Category: Praise
>
> **Task:** Classify this new review:
> Review: '[Paste customer review here]' -> Category:"

### Category: Data & Technical (SQL Generation)

**Use Case:** For analysts needing quick database queries.

> **Template:**
> "Create a MySQL query based on the schema below.
> **Table 1:** departments (Columns: DepartmentId, DepartmentName)
> **Table 2:** students (Columns: DepartmentId, StudentId, StudentName)
>
> **Request:** Create a query to list all students in the '[Department Name]' department."

### Category: HR & Recruitment

**Use Case:** Evaluating candidates or job descriptions.

> **Template:**
> "You are an expert HR recruiter.
> **Task:** Extract key skills from the job description below and list them in a bulleted list.
> **Job Description:** [Paste description here]"

## 6. FINAL CHECKLIST

Before sending a critical prompt, check:

- [ ] Did I give the AI a **Role**? (e.g., "Act as a marketing manager")
- [ ] Did I provide **Examples**? (If the format matters)
- [ ] Is the **Instruction** clear and positive? ("Do X" vs "Don't do Y")
